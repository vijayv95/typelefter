# Product Vision

## Why TypeLefter Exists

Writing software should empower writers, not own their work.

Many writing applications provide excellent tools for planning, organizing, and producing long-form content, but often rely on proprietary project formats or workflows that make it difficult for users to inspect, edit, or migrate their own data.

TypeLefter exists to provide a different approach.

Its goal is to become an open-source writing environment for authors, researchers, students, and anyone creating long-form content. TypeLefter is designed to evolve into a full fledged writing software, guided by openness, transparency, and user ownership.

The application should make writing easier without placing barriers between users and their work.

## Our Vision

To build the most open and transparent long-form writing environment available.

Every project created with TypeLefter should remain understandable and usable outside the application. Writers should never feel locked into a proprietary ecosystem or dependent on a single piece of software to access their own work.

TypeLefter should be a tool that users trust because it respects their ownership of both their writing and their creative process

## Who is it for?

TypeLefter is intended for anyone managing complex writing projects, including:

- Novelists
- World builders
- Researchers
- Technical writers
- Students
- Game designers
- Screenwriters
- Anyone producing long-form content

Although different users have different workflows, they all share a common need: organizing ideas while producing a ploished final manuscript.

## Core Values

### User Ownership

> Users always own their work.

TypeLefter should never prevent users from accessing, editing, or moving their content using other software.

### Openness

> The project should embrace open standards, open formats, and open development.

The software should be understandable not only to its users but also to its contributors.

### Transparency

> Nothing should be hidden simply because the application can hide it.

TypeLefter may simplify information, but it should always allow users to inspect, understand, and edit their own data.

### Longevity

> Writing often lasts longer than software.

Projects created today should remain accessible many years into the future. Design decisions should prioritize long-term accessibility over short-term convenience.

### Focus

> TypeLefter exists to help people write.

Every feature should support the writing process rather than distract from it. Complexity should only be introdoced when it provides meaningful value to writers.

## Guiding Principles

When making design decisions, we ask:

- Does this increase the user's ownership of their work?
- Does this make the project more transparent?
- Does this improve the writing experience?
- Does this preserve long-term accessibility?
- Does this solve a real problem for writers?

If the answer is "no", the feature should be reconsidered.

## Looking Forward

TypeLefter will continue to evolve through experimentation, community feedback, and practical experience. 

The goal is not to recreate another application feature-for-feature, but to learn from existing writing tools while developing an experience that reflects TypeLefter's own philosophy.

Every decision should move the project closer to becoming a writing platform that users can depends on, understand, and truly own.
